01 — The mechanism

Three tides,
one small coral.

No cloud, no camera, no tiny robot. Salt water, a mineral cartridge, and your listening, translated into a very slow current.

You link Spotify once, on a small local page. Driftwood reads tempo, repeats, and the hours you keep. Your playlists never leave the device; there is nowhere for them to go.

Input · tempo · repeats · hour of day

A quiet pump moves salt water across a mineral core. Your week sets the current: busier listening, a little more mineral. Skipped songs subtract nothing. Nothing here is punished.

Output · 0.2–0.6 mm of growth a day

Calcium carbonate settles, grain by grain, into a shape only your history could make. After twelve months the pump rests. The coral stops growing and becomes an archive of the year you had.

Patience · roughly 15 cm a year

02 — The object

A small device with
a long attention span.

It is not a speaker, a lamp, or a status symbol. It is a small wet object that changes when you are not looking. That turned out to be enough.

01 / Clock

It grows on its own clock.

About 0.4 mm a day. Check it weekly and something has changed; check it hourly and you will only see your own reflection. That is not a bug. That is the entire product.

02 / Interface

Nothing to check.

No app, no dashboard, no streak to protect. The coral is the only metric, and it does not send push notifications. Your phone will recover.

0 tabs open · 0 badges

03 / Hardware

Quiet, on purpose.

One warm light, one USB-C cable, no microphone. It cannot hear you. It only reads your listening, once a day, and then it goes back to being a dish.

04 / Material

Salt, not resin.

The coral is calcium carbonate, grown in a replaceable mineral cartridge. Send the cartridge back and we re-mineralise it. Nothing goes to landfill except the box, which is a box.

05 / Lifespan

One year, then still.

After twelve months the pump rests and the specimen is finished: a physical record of a year of sound. You can start another. You cannot restart this one.
